# HF-04 — Comparing Creatine Monohydrate Listings Without Ranking Them

## Hook

Two creatine listings may share a form and a serving number while differing in package details, seller language, and what the page actually verifies. A careful comparison describes the fields and leaves performance, safety, and personal fit outside the listing’s authority

## Context

This educational comparison uses THORNE Creatine, ASIN B07978VPPH, and Optimum Nutrition Creatine Monohydrate Powder, unflavored, 10 oz, ASIN B002DYIZEE. Rick’s readback recorded both pages on 2026-09-12 [S1][S2]. The goal is label and listing literacy, not a product choice, recommendation, or ranking.

The THORNE record describes micronized creatine monohydrate, unflavored powder, 5 g per serving, and 90 servings [S1]. The Optimum Nutrition record describes unflavored powder, 10 ounces, 5 g per serving, and 60 servings, with creatine monohydrate named in the listing fields [S2]. These are E2 observations tied to the recorded variants and date.

## Make the comparison table honest

Start with identity: ASIN, product title, flavor, package size, serving statement, and number of servings. Keep each row attached to its own source. Do not treat similar names as interchangeable, and do not use a related page’s details to complete a missing field.

Then add a column for verification status. For both examples, the full ingredient panel, physical package, lot, and independent certification record were not fully verified in the readback [S1][S2]. The Optimum Nutrition record also notes two UPC values in the listing field, so a package should not be selected from that field alone without a later label check.

## Separate shared ingredient from product evidence

The ODS fact sheet provides general information about exercise and performance supplements, including why ingredient, evidence, and context matter [S3]. A review in the project corpus can inform a general conversation about creatine protocols or studied populations [S4]. Neither source validates either finished product.

A shared ingredient does not establish identical formulation, manufacturing context, serving suitability, or personal experience. A matching 5 g field is a displayed serving statement, not a universal instruction. Keep any general research sentence attached to its studied population and protocol rather than moving it into a product conclusion.

## Attribute commercial language

The THORNE page uses language about lean muscle mass, endurance, cognitive function, cellular energy, and brain function [S1]. The Optimum Nutrition page uses language about muscle strength and power, muscle building, energy, recovery, comfort, safety, and a brand superlative [S2]. These are E2 claims from the seller or listing.

If a claim is relevant to explaining the page, write “the product listing states” and preserve the limitation. As of 2026-09-12, no completed standardized independent study confirming these claims for either SKU was located or verified within this review scope. This does not assert that research is absent everywhere. It means the reviewed material did not verify a completed standardized SKU study for this comparison.

A listing statement about banned-substance testing or participation in an Informed Choice program also remains an E2 declaration in this record [S2]. It should not be rewritten as clinical approval, universal safety, superior quality, or proof that the product will suit a particular person.

## Questions a comparison can answer

Which serving statement is displayed? Which package size and total servings are shown? Is the flavor recorded? Is the ingredient panel complete? Are directions and warnings visible? Which seller is shown? What was the access date? Which information remains not verified?

These questions produce a useful comparison without requiring a winner. Price, stock, reviews, and subscription terms are volatile marketplace fields. Review ratings are E3 perceptions, not evidence of performance or safety. Exclude them from any ranking argument.

## Keep personal decisions separate

A person considering a supplement may need to discuss health history, medicines, diet, and the reason for considering it with an appropriate professional. This article does not supply an individual serving, evaluate a condition, or decide between the two listings. Bring the exact current label if a professional conversation is appropriate.

## A compact comparison worksheet

Use one row for each ASIN and columns for form, flavor, package, serving statement, total servings, seller, date, and unverified fields. Put marketing language in a separate column and label it as E2. Finish with a sentence explaining that the table describes pages, not a person’s future experience. A worksheet can be precise without pretending to be a verdict.

## Keep the comparison reproducible

Another reviewer should be able to repeat the table from the URL, ASIN, variant, and access date. If a field changes, record the new snapshot separately. Do not silently rewrite the old observation. Reproducibility does not make a listing independent evidence, but it does make the editorial method easier to audit and correct.

## Closing

A sound creatine comparison is a record, not a verdict. Keep THORNE and Optimum Nutrition as dated E2 examples, separate ingredient research from SKU evidence, attribute commercial language, mark unknowns, and leave ranking out. The point is to read the page more accurately
